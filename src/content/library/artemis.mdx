## The one-paragraph verdict

Weir does the thing he does best: take a hostile environment — here a low-gravity, single-economy lunar colony — and reason out how it actually works, down to the welding and the air. Those passages are the book. The trouble is the story wrapped around them. The heist is a by-the-numbers caper with predictable beats, and the protagonist Jazz's voice tries so hard to be a wisecracking smartass that it lands flat and oddly samey. It's fast and short, which is its mercy — but it's clearly the lesser sibling of <ExternalCite title="The Martian" url="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18007564-the-martian" />.

## Who should read it

If you read <ExternalCite title="The Martian (Goodreads)" url="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18007564-the-martian" /> for the duct-tape-and-orbital-mechanics problem solving and didn't much care about characters, you'll get a smaller hit of the same. Skip it if you wanted the character growth or tighter plotting that a heist promises — it doesn't deliver either.

## Where it earned its place

It's pure off-duty reading, no platform tie-in. What it left me with is a reminder I value at work too: a system is only believable when you've actually traced how the pieces constrain each other. Weir's world holds together because he did that homework, even when the story didn't.

## Skip it if…

Pass if you've already decided Weir's smartass narrators grate on you, or if a flimsy plot ruins solid worldbuilding for you. There's better space fiction to spend an evening on.
