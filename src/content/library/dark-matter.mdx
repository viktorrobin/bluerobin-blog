## The one-paragraph verdict

Crouch takes a single sharp idea — what if every choice you didn't make is a world you could walk into — and runs it as hard as it'll go. The hook is genuinely good and the pacing is relentless; I read it in two sittings, which is the highest honest compliment I can pay a thriller. The reviews that praise it as a propulsive page-turner are right. The reviews that complain the characters are thin and the physics is hand-waved are also right: Jason exists mostly to keep the plot moving, and the back third leans on the premise harder than the premise can really bear. It works because it never slows down long enough for you to argue with it.

## Who should read it

Read it if you want a high-concept thriller you can finish on a flight and think about for a day afterward. It's a clean entry point for anyone who finds "real" sci-fi intimidating — the science is set dressing, not homework. Skip it if you want rigorous worldbuilding or characters with interior lives; this is plot machinery, and it doesn't pretend otherwise.

## Where it earned its place

No platform tie-in here — this is pure off-hours reading. What it gave me was a reminder that a single well-chosen constraint, executed without flinching, beats a sprawling idea executed timidly. That's a useful instinct whether you're designing a system or telling a story.

## Skip it if…

Pass if you bounce off thin characterization or want your multiverse fiction to actually respect its own rules. The premise is a slingshot, not a foundation, and by the end you can feel the seams.
