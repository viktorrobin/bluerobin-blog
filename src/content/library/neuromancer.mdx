This one's on the off-duty shelf. It's the novel that coined "cyberspace" and built the cyberpunk template everyone copied since — worth reading for that alone, even when the prose makes you work.
</Callout>

## The one-paragraph verdict

Gibson writes in a dense, neon-soaked style that throws you into the world without a map and refuses to explain itself. When it lands — the Sprawl, the matrix, Case jacking in — it's genuinely electric, and you can see why it took the Hugo, Nebula, and PKD all at once. The flip side is real: the plot lurches from place to place, several stretches are hard to track on a first read, and a few bits of the future feel dated now. I'd still take ambitious-and-disorienting over polished-and-forgettable. It earns its reputation, just not comfortably.

## Who should read it

Read it if you want to see the source code of an entire genre, or if you enjoy prose that trusts you to keep up. Skip it if you want clean exposition and a plot you can follow while half-distracted — this book actively resists that.

## Where it earned its place

It's pure off-hours reading, no platform tie-in. What it gave me was a reminder that vivid worldbuilding can carry a story further than tidy mechanics, and that "show, don't explain" is a posture, not just a rule. Mostly, though, I just enjoyed it.

## Skip it if…

If 1980s-flavored future-tech or deliberately murky plotting will frustrate you more than it intrigues you, pass — plenty of cyberpunk since owes Gibson a debt and reads easier. Read those instead.
