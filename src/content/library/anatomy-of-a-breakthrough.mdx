## The one-paragraph verdict

Alter's core claim is reassuring and probably correct: being stuck is the default state of any hard, long project, not a personal failure. The strength here is the storytelling — the anecdotes are well-chosen and the behavioral-science framing is easy to follow. The weakness is that he loses the thread. The "anatomy" promised by the title never quite cohers into a procedure, and the back half devolves into a sensible-but-generic list of things you already half-knew: take breaks, change context, lower the stakes. I closed it feeling validated and slightly under-equipped, which several reviewers flag too.

## Who should read it

Read it if you're mid-slog on something long and want a smart companion telling you the plateau is expected — that framing genuinely helps. Skip it if you already accept that stuck-ness is normal and want concrete techniques; the actionable density is low and you'll feel it.

## Where it earned its place

It gave me one durable mental model: treat a stall as a phase of the work rather than a verdict on me. That alone has cooled a few late-project panics. Beyond that it was pleasant airport-grade reading — engaging, frictionless, not demanding.

## Skip it if…

You want a tactical, do-this-next book. This is narrative reassurance with a thin operational layer; if you measure self-help by takeaways-per-page, it will frustrate you.
