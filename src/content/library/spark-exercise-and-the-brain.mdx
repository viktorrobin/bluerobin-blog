Ratey makes a genuinely persuasive case that exercise is brain maintenance, not just body maintenance. But this is an off-duty-shelf read for me, and you can get 80% of the payload from one good chapter.
</Callout>

## The one-paragraph verdict

The core argument lands: movement drives BDNF, neurogenesis, and the same neurotransmitter systems we try to manage with focus and mood. The Naperville school-fitness story alone is worth the price — a concrete, real-world result rather than a lab abstraction. Where it loses me is the padding: the book restates its thesis chapter after chapter, and Ratey routinely states correlational and animal-model findings with more certainty than the evidence carries. It reads like a great long-form article stretched to fill a book contract.

## Who should read it

Read it if you're an engineer who has quietly accepted a sedentary default and needs the mechanism spelled out to take it seriously — knowing *why* movement sharpens focus is more durable motivation than "exercise is good for you." Skip it if you already train regularly and just wanted the science; the marginal insight per page drops fast after the first third.

## Where it earned its place

This book didn't change a system — it changed a habit. It reframed my daily walk from "a break from work" to "part of the work," and that reframe stuck. I keep it on the shelf as the thing I hand people who treat exercise as the first item to cut when a deadline hits.

## Skip it if…
