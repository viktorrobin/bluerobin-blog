The first book that gives you a defensible, data-backed answer to "are we a good engineering org?" — four DORA metrics instead of vibes. Read Part 1; skim the rest.
</Callout>

## The one-paragraph verdict

What sets this apart from the usual DevOps shelf is that it is research, not testimony: Forsgren ran the State of DevOps survey through real statistical methods and came out with the four DORA metrics — deploy frequency, lead time, change-fail rate, time to restore. That gives you something rare in this field, a claim you can actually defend in a leadership meeting. The cost is that the book is front-loaded: Part 1 is the gold, Part 2 is a methods appendix that reads like one, and the whole thing repeats its own thesis more than it needs to. You can get 90% of the value from the first hundred pages. But that 90% reframed how I argue about engineering quality.

## Who should read it

Read it if you lead or influence delivery practice and keep losing the argument by anecdote — it hands you the vocabulary and the evidence. Platform and SRE teams choosing their first dashboard should start here. Skip it if you want a hands-on CI/CD implementation guide; this is the *why measure*, not the *how to build*.

## Where it earned its place

The DORA framing is exactly why I treat pipeline work as product work, not overhead — the argument I make in [engineering investment in the roadmap](/leadership/engineering-investment-product-roadmap/). And the four metrics are the reason BlueRobin's [GitHub Actions CI/CD](/articles/github-actions-cicd-dotnet/) optimises for lead time and low change-fail rate rather than raw build speed.

## Skip it if…
