## The one-paragraph verdict

DK does what DK does: the photography is genuinely striking, the cars are shot from every angle, and the index, glossary, and museum list make it easy to flip through. Where it earns the "situational" mark is depth — the per-car descriptions get repetitive marque to marque, and the selection leans hard toward European cars, so whole swathes of American and Japanese history feel thin. It's a book to browse, not to study. If you already know your Ferraris from your Facel Vegas, you'll find little new here beyond nice plates. As a confident, glossy overview, though, it does exactly what it sets out to do.

## Who should read it

Enthusiasts who want a giftable visual reference, design-history browsers, and anyone who enjoys cars as objects rather than engineering case studies. Skip it if you want restoration detail, buyer's-guide specifics, or a serious technical history — this is breadth and beauty, not depth.

## Where it earned its place

This one is purely off-duty. It gave me a quiet, screen-free way to wind down, and a better eye for why certain shapes from certain decades still look right. No platform tie-in, no professional excuse — I keep it because flipping through it is genuinely relaxing.

## Skip it if…

You're after a workshop manual, deep marque histories, or anything beyond European-centric highlights. As a learning tool it's shallow; as a thing to enjoy, it's fine.
