## The one-paragraph verdict

Wong shoots rain-slicked, neon-soaked Tokyo at night and grades it like a film colourist, which is exactly what he was before this. The tone and palette are genuinely top-notch — magenta-and-cyan frames that feel cinematic without tipping into cliché, most of the time. The honest catch: the images get samey. Once you've internalised the formula — empty street, reflected sign, one solitary figure — the back half stops surprising you, and the text is too sparse to fill the gap. It earns the 4.8 average on craft alone; it loses a point from me for repetition and for telling you almost nothing about how the look was made.

## Who should read it

Photographers and colourists who want a reference for a specific mood will get the most out of it, as will anyone who loves cyberpunk and neon-noir as an aesthetic. If you want technique, behind-the-scenes breakdowns, or a written argument about a city, look elsewhere — this is a picture book that mostly lets the pictures argue for themselves.

## Where it earned its place

It rewired how I look at colour. After this I started noticing what a deliberate two-tone grade does to a frame, and I'm more patient now about waiting for the right light instead of just taking the shot. Pure enjoyment, and a small permanent shift in how I see a street at night.

## Skip it if…
