## The one-paragraph verdict

Rozman teaches the way he does on YouTube: clear, patient, and genuinely good at sequencing the four pillars — openings, tactics, strategy, endgames — so a beginner isn't drowning. The production is the standout, clean diagrams and fun-fact boxes that keep dense material from feeling like a textbook. The honest catch, echoed across reviews, is that it describes and shows far more than it explains and explores; topics arrive, get a tidy summary, and move on. That's fine at 600 Elo and frustrating at 1300, which is roughly where the book stops being useful.

## Who should read it

Adult beginners and casual players parked under ~1200 — especially anyone who learned the moves from videos and wants a structured pass over the fundamentals. If you're already past 1300, or you want the *why* behind the patterns rather than the patterns themselves, look elsewhere; this isn't the book that gets you there.

## Where it earned its place

There's no platform tie-in here, and I won't pretend otherwise. Chess is my deliberate counterweight to staring at distributed systems all day — a board where the failure modes are mine alone and there's no on-call. This book gave me a cleaner mental checklist for the opening and middlegame, which is exactly the small, repeatable habit I wanted from it.
