## The one-paragraph verdict

Lovecraft is genuinely foundational: "The Call of Cthulhu" and "The Shadow Over Innsmouth" build a dread that almost nothing since has matched, and you can see the entire genre downstream of him. The atmosphere is the product — slow, suffocating, indifferent cosmos. But the prose is overwritten to the point of self-parody ("eldritch," "cyclopean," and "unnameable" arrive on schedule), the structure repeats itself across stories, and the period racism is real and unpleasant, not a footnote. I rate it below the Goodreads average because the craft hasn't aged as well as the ideas.

## Who should read it

Read it if you want to understand where cosmic horror came from, or if mood matters to you more than pace. Newcomers to eldritch horror get the best entry point here. Skip it if you need tight plotting or characters who feel like people — Lovecraft is interested in neither.

## Where it earned its place

It gave me a vocabulary for a specific feeling: the sense that a system is vastly larger and more indifferent than the part you can see. That mindset is oddly useful far from fiction. Mostly, though, it's here because a good Lovecraft story on a dark evening is still a pleasure, flaws and all.

## Skip it if…

Pass if dated, ornate prose grates on you, or if you can't read past the bigotry of the era. Both are present, and neither is small.
