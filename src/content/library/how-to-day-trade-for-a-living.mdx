## The one-paragraph verdict

Aziz is upfront about the part most trading books bury: the odds are bad, the psychology is the whole game, and capital plus discipline matter more than any setup. That candor, plus a genuinely smooth, example-driven writing style, is why it rates so well on Goodreads. But the actual mechanics are thin — it name-drops MACD, RSI and ATR without ever teaching them, so a true novice ends up googling the substance — and the whole book reads as an on-ramp to his chat room. You finish prepared to *start learning*, not equipped to trade.

## Who should read it

Read it if you are trading-curious and want an honest gut-check on expectations and risk before you sink time or money in. Skip it if you already understand position sizing and trading psychology, or if you want concrete, teachable strategy — the depth is not here.

## Where it earned its place

It is on the shelf as a reminder, not a reference. The one durable thing it gave me is a framing I apply well outside markets: define your max acceptable loss *before* you act, and treat discipline as the edge rather than the prediction. That is a decent lesson wrapped in a thin book.

## Skip it if…

Pass if you want a real strategy manual, or if you bristle at a book that doubles as an ad for the author's paid service. The good ideas here fit in a long blog post.
