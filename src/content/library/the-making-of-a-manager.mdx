## The one-paragraph verdict

Zhuo became a manager at twenty-five and writes from that exact vantage: the panic of your first one-on-one, the awkwardness of giving feedback to someone older than you. The structure around Purpose, People, and Process is clean, and her habit of narrating her own early mistakes makes the advice land without preaching. The recurring criticism in reviews is fair — much of it is foundational, and the anecdotes lean heavily on her Facebook design-org context, so the higher you climb the thinner it gets. But for the audience it targets, accessibility *is* the feature. I have never finished a management book this fast.

## Who should read it

Read it if you are about to become a manager, or did three months ago and feel underwater. Senior ICs weighing the switch will get an honest preview of the job. Skip it if you already run multiple teams — you will nod along but learn little new.

## Where it earned its place

The "right-size the team to the work, not your ego" instinct runs straight through [sizing teams in the copilot era](/leadership/sizing-teams-copilot-era/), where I argue smaller teams with stronger tooling now beat headcount. Zhuo's framing — a manager exists to improve outcomes, not to accumulate reports — is the assumption that whole piece rests on.
