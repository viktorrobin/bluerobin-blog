## The one-paragraph verdict

The pitch — deliberately starve yourself of cheap stimulation so hard work stops feeling unbearable by comparison — is genuinely useful, and Meurisse delivers it laser-focused and fast, which reviewers rightly praise. The problem is that there's one real idea here, and it's already in the title. The "dopamine" framing is pop-science shorthand that oversimplifies the actual neuroscience, and the back half pads a long-form blog post into a "book" with worksheets and restatement. I finished it in a sitting and remembered the premise, not the prose.

## Who should read it

Read it if you want the focus-versus-distraction idea explained quickly and don't mind paying for length you won't use. Skip it if you've ever read anything by Cal Newport, James Clear, or even a decent thread on the same topic — you already own this book's one insight, minus the filler.

## Where it earned its place

It gave me one durable habit: a deliberately boring first hour of the day, no feeds, no inbox, before any real work. That single change stuck, which is more than most productivity books leave behind. I keep it on the shelf as the example of a good idea that didn't need to be a book.

## Skip it if…

You want depth, real neuroscience, or anything you can't get from the cover and a focused afternoon. This is a reminder, not a manual — and you can get the reminder for free.
