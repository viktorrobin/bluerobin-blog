## The one-paragraph verdict

It's *The Martian*'s structure — one resourceful person, an impossible problem, solved one
testable hypothesis at a time — but with more heart and a genuinely great central
relationship. Weir's formula is unapologetically "smart person works the problem," and the
common criticism (thin prose, characters as plot devices) is fair. I did not care. If you
think in experiments and failure modes, this reads like home.

## Who should read it

Engineers, scientists, and anyone who liked *The Martian*. Skip it if you need literary
prose or deep character interiority — that's not the trade Weir is making.

## Where it earned its place

Nowhere in the platform, and that's the point: this is on the off-duty shelf. The honest
"professional" connection is only that its method — observe, hypothesise, test, repeat — is
the same loop the [Debug Agent](/debug-agent/) automates. Mostly I keep it here because it's
a reminder that the problem-solving instinct is supposed to be fun.

## Skip it if…

You're looking for hard literary fiction, or you bounce off "the science is the plot." For
everyone else who's ever enjoyed working a problem at 2am, it's an easy recommend.
