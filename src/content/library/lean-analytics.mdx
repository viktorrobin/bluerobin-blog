## The one-paragraph verdict

The core argument is sharp: at any moment there is exactly one metric you should obsess over, and it changes as you move through stages (empathy, stickiness, virality, revenue, scale). The discipline of picking *one* number and a line you must cross before you're allowed to care about the next one is genuinely useful, and the 30-odd case studies give you real benchmarks instead of hand-waving. The recurring criticism is fair, though — it's padded, the same ideas circle back two or three times, and some of the SaaS/e-commerce benchmarks already read as dated. Read it for the framework, not the page count.

## Who should read it

Founders, early PMs, and engineers who suddenly own a "north star" they didn't pick should read it cover to cover once. If you already run experiments against a single funnel metric and know your stage, you'll find most of it familiar — borrow the chapter on your current stage and skip the rest.

## Where it earned its place

The OMTM idea is exactly how I framed measurement on [Impact](/impact/): one load-bearing number per phase, with an explicit threshold before the next one matters — not a dashboard of forty equally-loud charts that no one acts on.

## Skip it if…

You want statistical rigor or causal-inference depth — this is a product-judgment book, not a stats book. Skip it too if you've already internalized lean-startup mechanics; the marginal idea-per-page here is low.
