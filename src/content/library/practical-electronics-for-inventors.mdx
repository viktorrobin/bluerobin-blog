## The one-paragraph verdict

This is the book people mean when they say "the one electronics book to own," and the breadth earns that reputation — components, theory, and circuits all in one volume, well illustrated and carefully explained. The flip side is that the first couple hundred pages are pure theory before you touch a real circuit, and the structure is uneven enough that it reads more like an encyclopedia than a course. The public 4.53 reflects how often it rescues someone mid-project; my 3.5 reflects that I never actually read it, I look things up in it. As a reference it's excellent. As a book, it's a slog if you try to go straight through.

## Who should read it

Hobbyists wiring up real boards, and software engineers like me who occasionally cross into hardware and want a single authoritative place to check a formula or a part. Skip it if you want a guided, project-first tutorial — Monk's smaller books do that job better, and this one will overwhelm you before you blink an LED.

## Where it earned its place

It gave me the confidence to stop guessing at circuits and actually understand what I was wiring. I don't read it; I keep it within arm's reach of a soldering iron and trust that whatever I half-remember from physics is in there, explained properly. That's a quiet kind of value — pure enjoyment of tinkering, backed by a book that always has the answer.

## Skip it if…

You want a quick start or a narrative read. The theory front-loading and reference-book density make it a poor first electronics book and a worse beach read.
