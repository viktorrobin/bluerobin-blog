## The one-paragraph verdict

`Spark Joy` is the illustrated sequel to `The Life-Changing Magic of Tidying Up`, and it's honest about being a how-to rather than a manifesto. The room-by-room walkthroughs and the genuinely good folding diagrams are where it shines — when I need to know what to do with a drawer of cables, the pictures beat any TED talk. The recurring complaint is fair: if you read the first book, a lot of this is restated, padded, and occasionally hard to take seriously (the talking-to-your-socks register is real). It works as reference, not as a sit-down read.

## Who should read it

Read it if you're in the middle of an actual declutter and want concrete, visual instructions for the awkward categories — paperwork, cables, sentimental junk. Skip it if you've already absorbed the KonMari method or you bounce off the animistic framing; you won't find a new system here, just more illustrations.

## Where it earned its place

It gave me one durable habit: the vertical fold, which I still use, and a default question — does this earn its place — that transfers cleanly to a backlog of half-finished side projects. No platform tie-in. It's just a tidy brain occasionally helping a tidy repo.

## Skip it if…

Pass if you want a single book — buy the original and stop there. And if "does this spark joy?" makes you wince rather than nod, this one will grate the whole way through.
