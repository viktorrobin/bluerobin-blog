## What it taught me

The constraint wasn't team *size* (Larson's 6–8 still held); it was **synchronisation**. And
the deeper issue was Conway's Law working against me: my team boundaries didn't match the
system's boundaries, so every change needed several teams to agree. The fix is to make team
boundaries follow the architecture, then let teams own their contracts and decide
independently.

## What I changed

- Reorganised teams around domain aggregates, each owning an API / NATS event contract, so most
  decisions stayed inside one team.
- Designed for the time zones on purpose: a defined overlap window for the few genuinely shared
  calls, async-by-default for everything else.
- Wrote down the decision protocol so "async" meant "decided in 24h", not "stalled".
