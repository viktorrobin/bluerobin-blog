## What it taught me

Engineering investment has to be a **sized, planned line in the roadmap**, not whatever's left over —
and it has to be argued in product terms (speed, reliability, cost), not virtue. Larson's work on
managing technical quality (and on where you choose to hold organizational risk) is exactly this:
quality is a continuous investment, not a one-off project.

## What I changed

- Reserved a standing fraction of capacity for engineering health, defended like any other commitment.
- Expressed each item as a product outcome (faster delivery, fewer incidents, lower cost).
- Put it on the *same* roadmap as features, sequenced — not a shadow backlog.
