## What it taught me

Larson's rule of thumb — teams of **roughly six to eight** — still holds, because team size is bound
by *coordination and cognitive load*, not lines of code. AI shifts where the work is (less drafting,
more reviewing, specifying, and verifying), so you size for the new bottleneck: review throughput,
context-sharing, and the cost of being wrong faster.

## What I changed

- Kept teams in the 6–8 band; resisted "AI means we can cut to N/2".
- Invested the freed-up capacity in review, testing, and eval/guardrails — the new constraint.
- Measured cognitive load and review latency, not raw output or lines.
