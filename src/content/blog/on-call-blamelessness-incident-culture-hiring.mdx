## What it taught me

Larson is direct about on-call and operational excellence resting on trust and autonomy. The
first question after an incident — "who broke it?" versus "what broke?" — tells everyone in the
room what kind of org they're in, and people calibrate their candour accordingly. Blame doesn't
just feel bad; it suppresses the information you need to actually get more reliable.

## What I changed

- Reset postmortems to be explicitly blameless, written async [PLACEHOLDER: ~48h later] rather
  than in the heat of the moment.
- Moved to SLOs and error budgets, so reliability became a *system* target, not a stick.
- Leaned on tooling-first diagnosis (the Debug-Agent approach) so the first artifact in an
  incident is evidence, not a name.
