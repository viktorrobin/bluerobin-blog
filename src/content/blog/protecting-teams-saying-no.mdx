## What it taught me

A big part of the job is to be a **filter, not a funnel** — to absorb organisational chaos and
protect the team's focus. Saying no (or "not now") to the wrong work is how you say yes to the
committed work. Larson treats saying no and managing up as core skills, not obstructionism.

## What I changed

- A single, visible intake + prioritisation path; no side-channel commitments.
- Pushed back with data (cost, trade-off, what it displaces) rather than a flat no.
- Said no *with an alternative* — a later slot, a smaller scope, or a different owner.
